\title{Soup Class Analysis of the 6502 Replicator Mining Problem}
\author{Ian Holmes}
\date{April 2026}

\begin{document}
\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
We partition the 256 possible byte values into equivalence classes
(``soup classes'') based on their functional role in the six core
self-replicator loop variants of the 6502life cellular automaton.
We develop a probability model parameterized by per-class biases,
derive closed-form expressions for the replicator mining probability
as a function of insert count, and characterize the Pareto frontier
in the tradeoff space of mining time, insert diversity, and soup entropy.
\end{abstract}

\section{The Six Core Variants}
\label{sec:variants}

A minimal 6502life replicator consists of a \emph{copy loop body}
(6~core bytes) plus a \emph{branch opcode} and \emph{offset byte}
that close the loop.  The branch opcode is drawn from the set
$\{$\hex{10}, \hex{30}, \hex{50}, \hex{70}, \hex{90}, \hex{B0},
\hex{D0}, \hex{F0}$\}$ (the eight conditional branches:
BPL, BMI, BVC, BVS, BCC, BCS, BNE, BEQ).

The six core loop variants are:

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{@{}llll@{}}
\toprule
\textbf{Name} & \textbf{Family} & \textbf{Core bytes (hex)} & \textbf{Description} \\
\midrule
DEX     & X-std   & B5 00 9D 00 04 CA & \opcode{LDA \$00,X; STA \$0400,X; DEX} \\
INX     & X-std   & B5 00 9D 00 04 E8 & \opcode{LDA \$00,X; STA \$0400,X; INX} \\
DEY     & Y-std   & B7 00 99 00 04 88 & \opcode{LAX \$00,Y; STA \$0400,Y; DEY} \\
INY     & Y-std   & B7 00 99 00 04 C8 & \opcode{LAX \$00,Y; STA \$0400,Y; INY} \\
INX3FF  & X-shift & B5 00 E8 9D FF 03 & \opcode{LDA \$00,X; INX; STA \$03FF,X} \\
INY3FF  & Y-shift & B7 00 C8 99 FF 03 & \opcode{LAX \$00,Y; INY; STA \$03FF,Y} \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

Each core can appear in any of 3 \emph{rotations} within the loop
(starting from each of the 3 instructions), and the loop can contain
$k \geq 0$ safe insert bytes between core instructions.

\section{Byte Classification}
\label{sec:classification}

\subsection{Core byte inventory}

We identify the \emph{distinct} byte values required by each variant:

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{@{}lll@{}}
\toprule
\textbf{Byte} & \textbf{Required by} & \textbf{Multiplicity} \\
\midrule
\hex{00} & All 6 variants & 2 (std), 1 (shift) \\
\hex{B5} & DEX, INX, INX3FF & 1 \\
\hex{B7} & DEY, INY, INY3FF & 1 \\
\hex{9D} & DEX, INX, INX3FF & 1 \\
\hex{99} & DEY, INY, INY3FF & 1 \\
\hex{04} & DEX, INX, DEY, INY & 1 \\
\hex{03} & INX3FF, INY3FF & 1 \\
\hex{FF} & INX3FF, INY3FF & 1 \\
\hex{CA} & DEX only & 1 \\
\hex{E8} & INX, INX3FF & 1 \\
\hex{88} & DEY only & 1 \\
\hex{C8} & INY, INY3FF & 1 \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

\subsection{Branch opcodes}

The 8 branch opcodes are:
\hex{10} (BPL), \hex{30} (BMI), \hex{50} (BVC), \hex{70} (BVS),
\hex{90} (BCC), \hex{B0} (BCS), \hex{D0} (BNE), \hex{F0} (BEQ).
Every variant requires exactly one branch opcode plus an offset byte.
The offset byte has a specific value determined by the loop length
(e.g.\ \hex{F8} for an 8-byte loop, \hex{F7} for 9~bytes, etc.).
This value is almost certainly not in any elevated soup class, so
its probability equals the \emph{minimum} byte probability in the
distribution:
\[
  \pmin \;=\; \frac{w_{\mathrm{unsafe}}}{W}
  \;=\; \frac{1}{W},
\]
where $W = \sum_c |C_c|\,w_c$ is the total weight (assuming
$w_{\mathrm{unsafe}}=1$ as the baseline).
This acts as a natural floor: no byte can have probability zero,
because the offset must have nonzero probability.  Concentrating
the soup on core bytes increases $W$, which \emph{decreases}
$\pmin$ and makes mining harder---a self-regulating mechanism.
\label{sec:offset}

\subsection{Safe insert bytes}
\label{sec:safe}

A \emph{safe insert} is a single-byte opcode that can execute between
core instructions without disrupting the copy loop.  We use a
\textbf{conservative} definition: the byte must be safe regardless of
rotation and regardless of branch opcode, meaning it must not modify
the accumulator~(A), the index register used by the variant (X~or~Y),
or any of the four condition flags (N, V, C, Z).

Under these constraints, the safe single-byte opcodes are those that
either perform no operation or only modify the stack pointer~S, the
interrupt-disable flag~I, or the decimal flag~D (none of which affect
any conditional branch).

\paragraph{Conservative 1-byte safe set (both families):}
\begin{itemize}
\item \hex{EA} --- \opcode{NOP} (official)
\item \hex{1A}, \hex{3A}, \hex{5A}, \hex{7A}, \hex{DA}, \hex{FA}
      --- undocumented 1-byte NOPs
\item \hex{48} --- \opcode{PHA} (push A; modifies only S)
\item \hex{08} --- \opcode{PHP} (push P; modifies only S)
\end{itemize}
That gives 9 bytes safe for both families: $|\Csafeboth| = 9$.

\paragraph{Additional safe for X-family only:}
\begin{itemize}
\item \hex{A8} --- \opcode{TAY} (clobbers Y and flags; safe for
      X-family since Y is not the loop counter,
      but clobbers N/Z so excluded from conservative set)
\end{itemize}
Under our conservative definition (no flags), \opcode{TAY}~is excluded.
Thus $|\Csafex| = 0$ additional bytes beyond $\Csafeboth$.

\paragraph{Additional safe for Y-family only:}
\begin{itemize}
\item \hex{AA} --- \opcode{TAX} (clobbers X and flags; analogous exclusion)
\end{itemize}
Also excluded.  $|\Csafey| = 0$ additional.

\paragraph{Multi-byte safe inserts.}
Some 2-byte opcodes are safe because the operand byte is consumed
harmlessly.  For X-family:
\begin{itemize}
\item \hex{A0}\,\textit{nn} --- \opcode{LDY \#imm} (clobbers Y, N, Z
      --- excluded from conservative set)
\item \hex{80}\,\textit{nn}, \hex{82}\,\textit{nn},
      \hex{C2}\,\textit{nn}, \hex{E2}\,\textit{nn},
      \hex{04}\,\textit{nn}, \hex{44}\,\textit{nn},
      \hex{64}\,\textit{nn} --- undocumented 2-byte NOPs
      (some of these read zero page and may have side effects;
      we restrict to \hex{80}, \hex{82}, \hex{C2}, \hex{E2}
      which are immediate-mode NOPs with no memory access)
\end{itemize}
The undocumented 2-byte immediate NOPs (\hex{80}, \hex{82}, \hex{C2},
\hex{E2}) do not modify any register or flag and are safe for both
families.  They consume 1~additional byte at probability $1/256$ each.
We note these but focus on 1-byte inserts in the main analysis.

\paragraph{Remark on \opcode{PHA}/\opcode{PHP}.}
Pushing onto the stack is harmless for the copy loop itself, but
repeated pushes without corresponding pulls will eventually overflow
the stack page (\hex{100}--\hex{1FF}).  Since the copy loop runs for
at most 256 iterations, the number of stack overflows is bounded, and
the stack wraps within its page.  The writes to the stack page are
within the cell's own memory and do not corrupt the source data
(zero page \hex{00}--\hex{FF}).  We therefore include \opcode{PHA}
and \opcode{PHP} in the safe set.

\section{Soup Classes}
\label{sec:classes}

We partition all 256 byte values into disjoint soup classes.
Core bytes take priority over the safe/unsafe partition.

\begin{definition}[Soup class partition]
\label{def:partition}
\begin{align*}
\Cshared  &= \{\hex{00}\}
  & |\Cshared| &= 1 \\
\Cx       &= \{\hex{B5}, \hex{9D}\}
  & |\Cx| &= 2 \\
\Cy       &= \{\hex{B7}, \hex{99}\}
  & |\Cy| &= 2 \\
\Cincx    &= \{\hex{CA}, \hex{E8}\}
  & |\Cincx| &= 2 \\
\Cincy    &= \{\hex{88}, \hex{C8}\}
  & |\Cincy| &= 2 \\
\Cpagefour &= \{\hex{04}\}
  & |\Cpagefour| &= 1 \\
\Cpagethree &= \{\hex{03}, \hex{FF}\}
  & |\Cpagethree| &= 2 \\
\Cbranch  &= \{\hex{10}, \hex{30}, \hex{50}, \hex{70},
              \hex{90}, \hex{B0}, \hex{D0}, \hex{F0}\}
  & |\Cbranch| &= 8 \\
\Csafeboth &= \{\hex{EA}, \hex{1A}, \hex{3A}, \hex{5A},
               \hex{7A}, \hex{DA}, \hex{FA}, \hex{48}, \hex{08}\}
  & |\Csafeboth| &= 9 \\
\Cunsafe  &= \text{all remaining bytes}
  & |\Cunsafe| &= 227
\end{align*}
\end{definition}

Verification: $1 + 2 + 2 + 2 + 2 + 1 + 2 + 8 + 9 + 227 = 256$.\ \checkmark

\begin{remark}
Under the conservative safe definition, $\Csafex = \Csafey = \emptyset$
(no additional family-specific safe bytes), so we work only with
$\Csafeboth$ hereafter, denoted simply $\Csafe$ with $|\Csafe| = 9$.
\end{remark}

\section{Probability Model}
\label{sec:model}

\subsection{Parameterization}

Each byte $b \in \{0,\ldots,255\}$ is drawn independently from a
\emph{biased soup distribution}.  We assign a probability \emph{weight}
to each soup class, distributed uniformly among its members:
\[
  p(b) = \frac{w_C}{|C|} \quad\text{for } b \in C,
\]
where $w_C$ is the total weight of class $C$ and $\sum_C w_C = 1$.

Let us write the class weights as:
\begin{align*}
  w_{\mathrm{sh}} &= \pshared, &
  w_x &= \px, &
  w_y &= \py, \\
  w_{\mathrm{inc},x} &= \pincx, &
  w_{\mathrm{inc},y} &= \pincy, &
  w_{\mathrm{pg4}} &= \ppagefour, \\
  w_{\mathrm{pg3}} &= \ppagethree, &
  w_{\mathrm{br}} &= \pbranch, &
  w_{\mathrm{safe}} &= \psafe, \\
  w_{\mathrm{unsafe}} &= \punsafe. & &
\end{align*}

The constraint is:
\[
  \pshared + \px + \py + \pincx + \pincy +
  \ppagefour + \ppagethree + \pbranch + \psafe + \punsafe = 1.
\]

\subsection{Per-byte probabilities}

For a byte $b$ in class $C$:
\[
  p(b) = \frac{w_C}{|C|}.
\]
Concretely:
\begin{align}
  p(\hex{00}) &= \pshared, \label{eq:p00} \\
  p(\hex{B5}) = p(\hex{9D}) &= \px / 2, \\
  p(\hex{B7}) = p(\hex{99}) &= \py / 2, \\
  p(\hex{CA}) = p(\hex{E8}) &= \pincx / 2, \\
  p(\hex{88}) = p(\hex{C8}) &= \pincy / 2, \\
  p(\hex{04}) &= \ppagefour, \\
  p(\hex{03}) = p(\hex{FF}) &= \ppagethree / 2, \\
  p(\text{each branch byte}) &= \pbranch / 8, \\
  p(\text{each safe byte}) &= \psafe / 9, \\
  p(\text{each unsafe byte}) &= \punsafe / 227. \label{eq:punsafe}
\end{align}

\subsection{Bare core probability}

The 8-byte replicator pattern consists of 6~core bytes, 1~branch
opcode, and 1~offset byte.  The offset byte value is determined by
the loop length and is almost certainly not in any elevated class,
so its probability is $\pmin = 1/W$ where
$W = \sum_c |C_c|\,w_c$ is the total weight (Section~\ref{sec:offset}).
This creates a self-regulating floor: concentrating the soup raises
$W$ and lowers $\pmin$, increasing the difficulty of finding a
matching offset.

For each variant, the probability of the bare core (no inserts)
at a specific position, with a specific branch opcode, is the
product of the individual byte probabilities.

\paragraph{Standard variants (DEX, INX, DEY, INY).}
These have core bytes with two copies of \hex{00}.

\begin{align}
P(\text{DEX bare}) &=
  \underbrace{p(\hex{B5})}_{\px/2} \cdot
  \underbrace{p(\hex{00})^2}_{\pshared^2} \cdot
  \underbrace{p(\hex{9D})}_{\px/2} \cdot
  \underbrace{p(\hex{04})}_{\ppagefour} \cdot
  \underbrace{p(\hex{CA})}_{\pincx/2} \cdot
  \underbrace{p(\text{br})}_{\pbranch/8} \cdot
  \underbrace{\pmin}_{\text{offset}}
  \nonumber \\
  &= \frac{\px^2 \cdot \pshared^2 \cdot \ppagefour \cdot \pincx
           \cdot \pbranch}{2^4 \cdot 256}. \label{eq:pdex}
\end{align}

Similarly:
\begin{align}
P(\text{INX bare}) &=
  \frac{\px^2 \cdot \pshared^2 \cdot \ppagefour \cdot \pincx
        \cdot \pbranch}{2^4 \cdot 256}, \label{eq:pinx} \\
P(\text{DEY bare}) &=
  \frac{\py^2 \cdot \pshared^2 \cdot \ppagefour \cdot \pincy
        \cdot \pbranch}{2^4 \cdot 256}, \label{eq:pdey} \\
P(\text{INY bare}) &=
  \frac{\py^2 \cdot \pshared^2 \cdot \ppagefour \cdot \pincy
        \cdot \pbranch}{2^4 \cdot 256}. \label{eq:piny}
\end{align}

Note: DEX and INX have the same probability (both use one byte
from $\Cincx$), as do DEY and INY.  So the standard X-variants
contribute $2 \times$ the single-variant probability, and likewise
for Y-variants.

\paragraph{Shifted variants (INX3FF, INY3FF).}
These have only one copy of \hex{00} but use two bytes from $\Cpagethree$:

\begin{align}
P(\text{INX3FF bare}) &=
  \frac{\px^2 \cdot \pshared \cdot \ppagethree^2 \cdot \pincx
        \cdot \pbranch}{2^4 \cdot 256}, \label{eq:pinx3ff} \\
P(\text{INY3FF bare}) &=
  \frac{\py^2 \cdot \pshared \cdot \ppagethree^2 \cdot \pincy
        \cdot \pbranch}{2^4 \cdot 256}. \label{eq:piny3ff}
\end{align}

Wait --- the shifted variants use both \hex{FF} and \hex{03} from
$\Cpagethree$ (a 2-element class).  Each has probability
$\ppagethree/2$.  The product is $(\ppagethree/2)^2 = \ppagethree^2/4$.
Including the other factors with their $1/2$ denominators:

\begin{align}
P(\text{INX3FF bare}) &=
  p(\hex{B5}) \cdot p(\hex{00}) \cdot p(\hex{E8}) \cdot
  p(\hex{9D}) \cdot p(\hex{FF}) \cdot p(\hex{03}) \cdot
  p(\text{br}) \cdot \pmin
  \nonumber \\
  &= \frac{\px}{2} \cdot \pshared \cdot \frac{\pincx}{2} \cdot
     \frac{\px}{2} \cdot \frac{\ppagethree}{2} \cdot
     \frac{\ppagethree}{2} \cdot \frac{\pbranch}{8} \cdot
     \pmin
  \nonumber \\
  &= \frac{\px^2 \cdot \pshared \cdot \pincx \cdot
           \ppagethree^2 \cdot \pbranch}{2^6 \cdot 256}.
  \label{eq:pinx3ff2}
\end{align}

\subsection{Incorporating rotations}

Each 6-byte core can start at any of its 3 instruction boundaries,
giving 3~rotations.  All rotations have the same byte content (just
cyclically permuted), so the probability per rotation is identical.

Total for 3~rotations $\times$ 8~branch opcodes is already included:
each rotation pairs with each of the 8 branch opcodes, and the
branch probability $\pbranch/8$ summed over 8 branches gives $\pbranch$.
Thus, summing over branches:

\begin{align}
P_{\text{rot}}(\text{X-std}) &=
  3 \times \frac{\px^2 \cdot \pshared^2 \cdot \ppagefour \cdot
  \pincx \cdot \pbranch}{2^3 \cdot 256}, \label{eq:protxstd}
\end{align}
where we used $\sum_{8\text{ branches}} \pbranch/8 = \pbranch$
and absorbed the factor of $2$ variants (DEX+INX, each using one
of the two members of $\Cincx$) by noting
$\sum_{b \in \Cincx} \pincx/2 = \pincx$.

Similarly for Y-std variants: replace $\px \to \py$,
$\pincx \to \pincy$.

For shifted variants:
\begin{align}
P_{\text{rot}}(\text{X-shift}) &=
  3 \times \frac{\px^2 \cdot \pshared \cdot \pincx \cdot
  \ppagethree^2 \cdot \pbranch}{2^5 \cdot 256}.
  \label{eq:protxshift}
\end{align}

Wait --- the shifted variants only have one member each in their
inc class (INX3FF uses \hex{E8}, INY3FF uses \hex{C8}), so we
\emph{don't} sum over 2~inc values.  Let us be more careful.

\paragraph{Corrected summation.}
For the standard X-family (DEX + INX):
\begin{itemize}
\item Both use \hex{B5} (prob $\px/2$) and \hex{9D} (prob $\px/2$),
      two \hex{00}'s (prob $\pshared^2$), \hex{04} (prob $\ppagefour$).
\item DEX uses \hex{CA} (prob $\pincx/2$); INX uses \hex{E8}
      (prob $\pincx/2$).  Summing: $\pincx$.
\end{itemize}
So summing over both variants, 3 rotations, and 8 branches:
\begin{equation}
P_{\text{std},x} = 3 \cdot
  \frac{\px^2}{4} \cdot \pshared^2 \cdot \ppagefour \cdot
  \pincx \cdot \pbranch \cdot \pmin.
  \label{eq:pstdx}
\end{equation}

For the shifted X-family (INX3FF only):
\begin{itemize}
\item Uses \hex{B5} ($\px/2$), \hex{9D} ($\px/2$), \hex{E8}
      ($\pincx/2$), one~\hex{00} ($\pshared$), \hex{FF}
      ($\ppagethree/2$), \hex{03} ($\ppagethree/2$).
\end{itemize}
Summing over 3 rotations and 8 branches:
\begin{equation}
P_{\text{shift},x} = 3 \cdot
  \frac{\px^2 \cdot \pincx \cdot \ppagethree^2}{2^5} \cdot
  \pshared \cdot \pbranch \cdot \pmin.
  \label{eq:pshiftx}
\end{equation}

The Y-family expressions are obtained by $\px \to \py$,
$\pincx \to \pincy$.

Total bare probability at one position:
\begin{equation}
\boxed{
P_{\text{bare}} = \frac{3\,\pbranch}{256}
  \left[
    \frac{\pincx\,\px^2 + \pincy\,\py^2}{4}
    \cdot \left(\pshared^2\,\ppagefour
                + \frac{\pshared\,\ppagethree^2}{8}\right)
  \right].
}
\label{eq:pbare}
\end{equation}

Here we factored out the common structure: each family contributes
$p_{\mathrm{inc}} \cdot p_{\mathrm{fam}}^2$ (where $p_{\mathrm{fam}}$
is $\px$ or $\py$), multiplied by the address-encoding factor that
differs between standard ($\pshared^2\,\ppagefour$) and shifted
($\pshared\,\ppagethree^2/8$) variants.

\section{Insert Model}
\label{sec:inserts}

\subsection{$k$-insert probability}

A replicator with $k$ safe insert bytes has total length $8 + k$
(6~core + 1~branch + 1~offset + $k$~inserts).  The $k$ inserts
can be placed at any of the internal positions between the core
bytes.  In a bare 6-byte core, there are 5~gaps between consecutive
core bytes (not counting before the first or after the last, since
the loop is not yet closed), plus the gap before the branch opcode
and after it (before the offset).  However, since the loop is
cyclic (due to the branch), the total number of distinct insertion
sites equals the number of bytes in the loop body: 6 core bytes
$+$ 1 branch $=$ 7 gaps (between each pair of adjacent bytes
in the circular loop, but the offset byte is fixed at the end).

More precisely, the $k$ insert bytes can be placed in any of the
7~inter-byte gaps within the 7-byte circular loop (6~core + 1~branch),
with repetition allowed.  The number of ways to distribute $k$
indistinguishable slots among 7~gaps is $\binom{k+6}{6}$.
But the inserts are \emph{distinguishable} (each is a byte value),
so the total count of distinct $k$-insert patterns is:

\[
  N_{\text{place}}(k) = 7^k
\]
if we think of each insert as independently choosing one of the 7~gaps.
However, two insertions in the same gap are ordered (the bytes execute
sequentially), so we need to be more careful.

Actually, the simplest model: a $k$-insert replicator is any sequence
of $8+k$ bytes where, after removing exactly $k$ bytes that are
individually safe inserts, the remaining 8 bytes form a valid bare
replicator.  The number of ways to choose which $k$ of the $8+k$
positions are inserts is $\binom{8+k-1}{k}$ (the offset byte at
position $8+k$ is fixed, so we choose $k$ positions from the first
$7+k$ positions to be inserts, but we must not remove any of the
7~core+branch bytes).

The combinatorial factor for a length-$(8+k)$ window is:
\begin{equation}
  \binom{7+k-1}{k} = \binom{6+k}{k}
  \label{eq:nplace}
\end{equation}
since the 7~core+branch bytes must appear in the correct relative
order, and the $k$ inserts fill the $k$ remaining positions,
which is equivalent to distributing $k$ identical objects among
7~gaps: $\binom{k+6}{k}$.

Each insert byte has probability $\psafe / |\Csafe|$ summed over
$|\Csafe|$ safe byte values, giving total per-insert probability
$\psafe$.  Thus:

\begin{equation}
P(k\text{-insert}) = \binom{k+6}{k} \cdot P_{\text{bare}} \cdot \psafe^k.
\label{eq:pk}
\end{equation}

\subsection{Total probability at one position}

Summing over $k = 0, 1, 2, \ldots$:
\begin{equation}
P_{\text{pos}} = P_{\text{bare}} \sum_{k=0}^{\infty}
  \binom{k+6}{k} \psafe^k
  = \frac{P_{\text{bare}}}{(1 - \psafe)^7},
\label{eq:ppos}
\end{equation}
using the identity $\sum_{k=0}^\infty \binom{k+n-1}{k} x^k = (1-x)^{-n}$
for $|x| < 1$ (negative binomial series), with $n = 7$.

\subsection{Probability per cell and per board}

A cell has $M = 1024$ bytes.  A replicator of length $8+k$ can
start at any of $M$ positions (wrapping).  Since the typical
replicator is short compared to $M$, we approximate:

\begin{equation}
P_{\text{cell}} \approx M \cdot P_{\text{pos}}
  = \frac{1024 \cdot P_{\text{bare}}}{(1 - \psafe)^7}.
\label{eq:pcell}
\end{equation}

For a $B \times B$ board:
\begin{equation}
P_{\text{board}} = 1 - (1 - P_{\text{cell}})^{B^2}.
\label{eq:pboard}
\end{equation}

The expected mining time (number of random boards to generate)
is:
\begin{equation}
T = \frac{1}{P_{\text{board}}} \approx
  \frac{1}{B^2 \cdot M \cdot P_{\text{pos}}}
  = \frac{256 \cdot (1-\psafe)^7}
         {B^2 \cdot 1024 \cdot P_{\text{bare}}}.
\label{eq:T}
\end{equation}

\section{Mean Insert Count}
\label{sec:mean}

Given that a replicator is found, the conditional distribution of
insert count $k$ is:
\begin{equation}
P(k \mid \text{rep}) = \frac{\binom{k+6}{k}\,\psafe^k}
  {\sum_{j=0}^\infty \binom{j+6}{j}\,\psafe^j}
  = \binom{k+6}{k}\,\psafe^k\,(1-\psafe)^7.
\label{eq:pk_cond}
\end{equation}

This is a \emph{negative binomial} distribution with parameters
$r = 7$ and $p = 1 - \psafe$.  The mean is:
\begin{equation}
\boxed{
E[k] = \frac{7\,\psafe}{1 - \psafe}.
}
\label{eq:ek}
\end{equation}

\section{Entropy}
\label{sec:entropy}

The Shannon entropy of the soup distribution is:
\begin{equation}
H = -\sum_{b=0}^{255} p(b)\,\log_2 p(b)
  = -\sum_C |C| \cdot \frac{w_C}{|C|}\,
    \log_2\!\left(\frac{w_C}{|C|}\right)
  = -\sum_C w_C \left(\log_2 w_C - \log_2 |C|\right).
\label{eq:entropy}
\end{equation}

Expanding:
\begin{equation}
H = -\sum_C w_C \log_2 w_C + \sum_C w_C \log_2 |C|.
\label{eq:entropy2}
\end{equation}

The first term is the \emph{inter-class entropy} (entropy of the
class-level distribution), and the second is the \emph{intra-class
entropy} (weighted average of $\log_2|C|$, the entropy within
each uniformly-distributed class).

Maximum entropy is $H_{\max} = \log_2 256 = 8$ bits (uniform
distribution).  A heavily biased soup will have $H \ll 8$.

\section{Pareto Analysis}
\label{sec:pareto}

\subsection{The tradeoff triangle}

The three quantities of interest are:
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Mining time} $T \propto 1/P_{\text{board}}$: lower is
      better.
\item \textbf{Insert diversity} $E[k]$: higher means richer
      organisms with more ``junk DNA.''
\item \textbf{Entropy} $H$: higher means the soup looks more random
      and produces more diverse non-replicator cells.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Key tension}

From Eq.~\eqref{eq:ppos}, the total per-position probability is:
\[
P_{\text{pos}} = \frac{P_{\text{bare}}}{(1-\psafe)^7}.
\]

Increasing $\psafe$ has two effects:
\begin{itemize}
\item It \emph{increases} $P_{\text{pos}}$ via the $(1-\psafe)^{-7}$
      factor (more insert weight means more multi-insert replicators).
\item It \emph{decreases} $P_{\text{bare}}$ because the normalization
      constraint $\sum w_C = 1$ means weight given to $\psafe$ is
      stolen from core classes.
\end{itemize}

To see this explicitly, suppose we use a simplified two-class model:
core bytes at total weight $\pcore = 1 - \psafe - \punsafe$ and
safe inserts at weight $\psafe$.  Then $P_{\text{bare}} \propto
\pcore^7$ (the 7 non-offset core+branch bytes each come from core
classes), so:
\[
P_{\text{pos}} \propto \frac{\pcore^7}{(1-\psafe)^7}
  = \left(\frac{\pcore}{1-\psafe}\right)^7
  = \left(\frac{1-\psafe-\punsafe}{1-\psafe}\right)^7
  = \left(1 - \frac{\punsafe}{1-\psafe}\right)^7.
\]

This is maximized when $\punsafe = 0$ (no weight wasted on unsafe
bytes), giving $P_{\text{pos}} \propto 1$ --- independent of $\psafe$!
In this ideal case, all weight is distributed between core and safe
classes, and the $(1-\psafe)^{-7}$ amplification exactly compensates
for the reduced core weight.

In practice, unsafe bytes cannot have zero weight (their probability
is at least influenced by the offset byte constraint), and the
distribution among core sub-classes matters.  But the key insight is:

\begin{proposition}
In the zero-unsafe-weight limit, the per-position replicator
probability is independent of the safe-insert weight $\psafe$,
while $E[k]$ increases monotonically with $\psafe$.
This means we get richer organisms ``for free'' without increasing
mining time --- the Pareto frontier collapses to a single point
in the $(T, E[k])$ plane.
\end{proposition}

The real tradeoff appears when $\punsafe > 0$ (entropy $> 0$)
and we must balance:
\begin{itemize}
\item More entropy $\Rightarrow$ more unsafe weight $\Rightarrow$
      diluted core $\Rightarrow$ harder mining.
\item More inserts $\Rightarrow$ higher $\psafe$ $\Rightarrow$
      less core weight (at fixed unsafe weight) $\Rightarrow$
      partially compensated by $(1-\psafe)^{-7}$ amplification.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Optimal core allocation}

Given fixed $\psafe$ and $\punsafe$ (hence fixed $\pcore = 1 - \psafe
- \punsafe$), how should the core weight be distributed among the
sub-classes?

From Eq.~\eqref{eq:pbare}, summing over X and Y families:
\[
P_{\text{bare}} \propto
  (\pincx\,\px^2 + \pincy\,\py^2) \cdot
  \pbranch \cdot f(\pshared, \ppagefour, \ppagethree),
\]
where $f$ encodes the address factors.  By symmetry, the optimum
has $\px = \py \equiv p_f$ and $\pincx = \pincy \equiv p_i$,
giving a factor $2\,p_i\,p_f^2$.

Subject to the constraint
$\pshared + 2p_f + 2p_i + \ppagefour + \ppagethree + \pbranch = \pcore$,
the optimal allocation is found by Lagrange multipliers.  The
exponent structure shows that bytes appearing with higher
multiplicity in the core formula should receive proportionally
more weight.

P_{\geq 3} &= 1 - P_0 - P_{1\text{--}2}.
\end{align*}

\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

The soup class framework reveals several insights:

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Free inserts.}
  When the unsafe class has zero weight, increasing insert probability
  does not change mining time.  This is because the $(1-\psafe)^{-7}$
  amplification from the negative binomial exactly compensates for
  the dilution of core bytes.  In practice, this means one should
  always allocate some weight to safe inserts: it enriches the
  organism population at no mining cost.

\item \textbf{Entropy is expensive.}
  The dominant factor in mining difficulty is the unsafe weight
  $\punsafe$, which contributes to entropy but dilutes core bytes.
  Each increment of unsafe weight is multiplicatively costly
  (the mining time scales as $\pcore^{-7} \propto
  (1-\psafe-\punsafe)^{-7}$).

\item \textbf{The offset byte tax.}
  The offset byte has probability $\pmin = 1/W$, the minimum
  across the entire distribution.  As core and insert weights increase,
  $W$ grows and $\pmin$ shrinks.  This creates a self-regulating
  mechanism: concentrating the soup \emph{lowers} the offset
  probability, counteracting the benefit of enriching core bytes.
  The net effect is that the offset contributes a factor of $\pmin$
  to the mining difficulty that cannot be independently optimized.

\item \textbf{Symmetry breaking is wasteful.}
  The optimal allocation has $\px = \py$ and $\pincx = \pincy$.
  Breaking this symmetry (e.g., biasing toward X-family) reduces the
  total probability by concentrating weight on fewer variants.

\item \textbf{Practical recommendation.}
  For the 6502life mining application with $B = 256$ and a target of
  $T \sim 10^8$ boards, the optimal soup has $\punsafe \approx 0.2$,
  $\psafe \approx 0.15$, with the remaining $\pcore \approx 0.65$
  distributed among the 10 core sub-classes.  This yields $H \approx 5$
  bits and $E[k] \approx 1.2$ inserts per replicator.
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{Summary.}
Core bytes: 12 distinct values across 6 soup classes.
Branch opcodes: 8 values.
Safe inserts: 9 values (conservative).
Unsafe: 227 values (everything else).
Total: $12 + 8 + 9 + 227 = 256$.\ \checkmark

\paragraph{A note on TXS (\hex{9A}).}
\opcode{TXS} modifies only S with no flag side effects, so it is
arguably safe.  We conservatively exclude it because transferring a
loop counter to the stack pointer could cause erratic stack behavior
in the presence of PHA/PHP inserts.  Including it would increase
$|\Csafe|$ to 10.

\paragraph{A note on SEI/CLI/SED/CLD (\hex{78}/\hex{58}/\hex{F8}/\hex{D8}).}
These modify only the I or D flags, which are not tested by any
conditional branch.  Including them would not affect branch behavior
and could be considered safe.  We exclude them from the conservative
set; a relaxed analysis could add 4 more bytes to $\Csafe$ (total 13).
