\begin{abstract}
We derive closed-form distance formulas for estimating evolutionary divergence
between cells in the 6502life cellular automata system, where replication
proceeds through a hierarchical noisy copy channel. The noise model operates
at three levels---cell, byte, and bit---producing correlated errors within
bytes. We show that the per-byte Hamming distance follows a mixture of two
binomials, with eigenvalues given by the Krawtchouk polynomials of the
underlying binary symmetric channel. We derive maximum likelihood estimators
at three levels of resolution and validate them against Monte Carlo simulations.
\end{abstract}

\section{The Noisy Copy Channel}

In 6502life, cell replication proceeds via the \texttt{copyCellWithNoise}
mechanism, which copies $M = 1024$ bytes from a source cell to a destination
cell with three levels of noise:

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Cell level}: With probability $p_{\mathrm{CN}}$, the entire
  destination cell is filled with random bytes (all $8M$ bits i.i.d.\
  $\mathrm{Bernoulli}(1/2)$).
\item \textbf{Byte level}: For each byte, with probability $p_{\mathrm{BN}}$
  (given the cell was not randomized), the byte is replaced by a random byte.
\item \textbf{Bit level}: For each bit, with probability $\varepsilon =
  p_{\mathrm{bit}}$ (given neither cell nor byte noise fired), the bit is
  replaced by a random bit.
\end{enumerate}

The default parameters are $p_{\mathrm{CN}} = 0$, $p_{\mathrm{BN}} = 0$,
$\varepsilon = 0.001$.

\subsection{Effective rates}

The \emph{per-byte randomization rate} (probability that all 8 bits in a byte
are simultaneously randomized):
\begin{equation}
r = p_{\mathrm{CN}} + p_{\mathrm{BN}}(1 - p_{\mathrm{CN}})
\end{equation}

The \emph{effective per-bit randomization rate}:
\begin{equation}
\varepsilon_{\mathrm{eff}} = 1 - (1 - p_{\mathrm{CN}})(1 - p_{\mathrm{BN}})(1 - \varepsilon)
\end{equation}

\section{Bit-Level Distance (Binary Jukes--Cantor)}

Each bit evolves under a 2-state symmetric Markov chain. Per copy event,
a bit is randomized (replaced by a fair coin flip) with probability
$\varepsilon_{\mathrm{eff}}$, and faithfully copied otherwise.

\begin{proposition}
After $T$ copy events on the tree path between two leaves (total evolutionary
distance), the probability that a single bit differs is
\begin{equation}
p_{\mathrm{bit}}(T) = \tfrac{1}{2}\bigl(1 - (1-\varepsilon_{\mathrm{eff}})^T\bigr)
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The per-bit pairwise comparison follows a 2-state Markov chain with
transition rate $\varepsilon_{\mathrm{eff}}/2$ in each direction (same $\to$
different and different $\to$ same), since randomization produces a match with
probability $1/2$ regardless of the current state. Starting from ``same''
at $T=0$:
\[
P(\text{diff after }T) = \frac{1}{2}\bigl(1 - (1-\varepsilon_{\mathrm{eff}})^T\bigr)
\]
This is the standard binary Jukes--Cantor model.
\end{proof}

Inverting:
\begin{equation}
\boxed{T = \frac{\log(1 - 2\,p_{\mathrm{bit}})}{\log(1 - \varepsilon_{\mathrm{eff}})}}
\end{equation}
Saturation: $p_{\mathrm{bit}} \to 1/2$ as $T \to \infty$.

\section{The 9-State Byte Hamming Model}

\subsection{Markov chain on Hamming distance}

Consider a single byte position. The \emph{pairwise Hamming distance}
$h \in \{0,1,\ldots,8\}$ (number of bit positions where two corresponding
bytes differ) evolves as a 9-state Markov chain.

Per copy event on one lineage:
\begin{itemize}
\item With probability $r$: all 8 bits are randomized. Regardless of the
  current state, each bit independently becomes ``same'' or ``different''
  with equal probability. The new $h \sim \Bin(8, 1/2)$.
\item With probability $1-r$: each of the $h$ ``different'' bits repairs
  (becomes ``same'') with probability $\varepsilon/2$, and each of the
  $8-h$ ``same'' bits breaks (becomes ``different'') with probability
  $\varepsilon/2$.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Eigenvalues via Krawtchouk polynomials}

The transition matrix $P$ for this chain has the structure
\[
P = (1-r)\,Q + r\,\mathbf{1}\,\boldsymbol{\pi}^\top
\]
where $Q$ is the bit-level-only transition matrix and
$\boldsymbol{\pi} = (\Bin(h;\,8,\,1/2))_{h=0}^8$ is the stationary distribution.

The matrix $Q$ describes 8 independent binary symmetric channels projected
onto their Hamming weight. Its eigenvectors are the Krawtchouk polynomials
$K_k(h;\,8,\,1/2)$ with eigenvalues $\mu_k = (1-\varepsilon)^k$ for
$k = 0,\ldots,8$.

Since $\boldsymbol{\pi}^\top K_k = 0$ for $k \geq 1$ (orthogonality), the
eigenvalues of $P$ are:
\begin{equation}
\lambda_0 = 1, \qquad \lambda_k = (1-r)(1-\varepsilon)^k \quad\text{for } k = 1,\ldots,8
\end{equation}

\subsection{Closed-form distribution: mixture of two binomials}

\begin{theorem}
The distribution of per-byte Hamming distance $h$ between two cells separated
by $T$ copy events on the tree, starting from $h=0$, is
\begin{equation}
\boxed{\pi_T(h) = (1-s)\,\Bin(h;\,8,\,\tfrac{1}{2}) + s\,\Bin(h;\,8,\,p)}
\label{eq:mixture}
\end{equation}
where
\begin{align}
s &= (1-r)^T & &\text{(probability of no byte-randomization on the path)} \\
p &= \tfrac{1}{2}\bigl(1-(1-\varepsilon)^T\bigr) & &\text{(per-bit mismatch from bit-noise alone)}
\end{align}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Using the Krawtchouk generating function
$\sum_{k=0}^{n} z^k K_k(h;\,n) = (1-z)^h(1+z)^{n-h}$
and the spectral decomposition starting from $h_0=0$ (where $K_k(0;\,8) = \binom{8}{k}$):
\begin{align}
\pi_T(h) &= \frac{\binom{8}{h}}{256}\sum_{k=0}^{8} \lambda_k^T\, K_k(h) \\
&= \frac{\binom{8}{h}}{256}\Bigl[1 + s\Bigl(\sum_{k=0}^{8} z^k K_k(h) - 1\Bigr)\Bigr]
\qquad\text{where } z = (1-\varepsilon)^T \\
&= \frac{\binom{8}{h}}{256}\bigl[1 - s + s\,(1-z)^h(1+z)^{8-h}\bigr]
\end{align}
Setting $p = (1-z)/2$:
\[
\pi_T(h) = (1-s)\frac{\binom{8}{h}}{256} + s\,\binom{8}{h}p^h(1-p)^{8-h}
\]
which is the mixture~\eqref{eq:mixture}.
\end{proof}

\textbf{Interpretation.} With probability $s$, the byte was never hit by
byte-level or cell-level noise, so $h$ arises purely from bit-noise
accumulation: $h \sim \Bin(8,p)$. With probability $1-s$, the byte was
randomized at some point, erasing all signal: $h \sim \Bin(8,1/2)$.

\subsection{Moments}

The mean of $\pi_T$ is
\[
\E[h] = (1-s)\cdot 4 + s\cdot 8p = 4\bigl(1 - (1-\varepsilon_{\mathrm{eff}})^T\bigr) = 8\,p_{\mathrm{bit}}(T)
\]
which is identical to the bit-level formula. The variance, however, is
inflated relative to a single binomial when $r > 0$, due to the mixture.

\subsection{Special cases}

\textbf{Default parameters} ($r = 0$, $s = 1$): The mixture collapses to
a single $\Bin(8,p)$, and the MLE of $T$ is the bit-level formula applied
to $\hat{p} = \bar{h}/8$ (the sample mean of the histogram divided by 8).

\textbf{Byte match/mismatch}: Setting $h = 0$ recovers the byte-level
match probability:
\[
\pi_T(0) = \frac{1-s}{256} + s\,(1-p)^8
= \frac{1-s}{256} + s\,\Bigl[\frac{1+(1-\varepsilon)^T}{2}\Bigr]^8
\]

\section{Distance Estimation}

\subsection{From bit mismatch fraction}

Given observed fraction $\hat{p}_{\mathrm{bit}}$ of differing bits across
$L$ bytes (i.e., $8L$ bit comparisons):
\begin{equation}
\hat{T}_{\mathrm{bit}} = \frac{\log(1 - 2\hat{p}_{\mathrm{bit}})}{\log(1 - \varepsilon_{\mathrm{eff}})}
\end{equation}
This is always available and is closed-form.

\subsection{From byte mismatch fraction}

Given fraction $\hat{p}_{\mathrm{byte}}$ of differing bytes:
\begin{equation}
\hat{T}_{\mathrm{byte}} = \frac{\log\bigl(2(1-\hat{p}_{\mathrm{byte}})^{1/8} - 1\bigr)}{\log(1-\varepsilon)}
\qquad\text{(for } r = 0\text{)}
\end{equation}
For $r > 0$, numerical bisection on the forward model.

\subsection{From the 9-state histogram (MLE)}

Given observed histogram $(n_0, \ldots, n_8)$ with $\sum n_h = L$:

\textbf{Case $r = 0$:} The distribution is $\Bin(8,p)$, so $\hat{p} = \bar{h}/8$
is sufficient. The MLE is the same as the bit-level formula.

\textbf{Case $r > 0$:} The distribution is a 2-component binomial mixture
with parameters $(s, p)$ coupled through $T$. The unconstrained MLE of
$(s, p)$ can be obtained via EM:

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{E-step:} Posterior weight for the signal component:
\[
w_h = \frac{s\,\Bin(h;\,8,\,p)}{(1-s)\,\Bin(h;\,8,\,1/2) + s\,\Bin(h;\,8,\,p)}
\]

\noindent\textbf{M-step} (closed form):
\begin{align}
\hat{s} &= \frac{\sum_h n_h\, w_h}{L} \\
\hat{p} &= \frac{\sum_h h\, n_h\, w_h}{8\sum_h n_h\, w_h}
\end{align}

After convergence, two independent distance estimates:
\begin{align}
T_p &= \frac{\log(1-2\hat{p})}{\log(1-\varepsilon)} & &\text{(from bit-noise accumulation)} \\
T_s &= \frac{\log\hat{s}}{\log(1-r)} & &\text{(from byte-noise accumulation)}
\end{align}

Under the model, $T_p \approx T_s$. The discrepancy $|T_p - T_s|$ is a
diagnostic for model violations (e.g., mutations from sources other than the
noisy copy channel).

\subsection{Goodness of fit}

The Pearson chi-squared statistic
\[
\chi^2 = \sum_{h=0}^{8} \frac{(n_h - L\,\hat{\pi}_T(h))^2}{L\,\hat{\pi}_T(h)}
\]
tests whether the observed histogram is consistent with the copy-noise model.
Under the null hypothesis, $\chi^2 \sim \chi^2_{d}$ where $d$ is the number
of bins with expected count $\geq 5$, minus the number of fitted parameters.

\subsection{Histogram diagnostics}

For all three spreaders, the chi-squared goodness-of-fit statistic strongly
rejects the pure copy-noise model ($\chi^2 \gg$ critical value at any
significance level). The observed histograms are bimodal:
\begin{itemize}
\item Most bytes are identical ($h = 0$): faithful copies
\item A fraction are completely unrelated ($h \sim \Bin(8, 1/2)$): overwrites
  from unrelated cells
\end{itemize}

This is consistent with a mixture model where the ``noise'' comes from
\emph{partial overwrites and cross-contamination} (random cells writing into
each other's code regions), not from the per-bit noise channel. The 9-state
histogram model provides a natural diagnostic for detecting these non-copy
mutation sources.

The BRK spreader, which uses the engine's built-in noisy copy (operating on
all 1024 bytes atomically), should in principle match the model better than
the LDA/STA spreaders. In practice, cross-contamination from random cells
still dominates the mutation signal.
